# Twilight of a god: mathematics in an age of AI

*4 August 2026*

I am too young to remember 9/11 but I will always remember where I was when the Jacobian conjecture fell. It was the morning after the World Cup final, and I logged into Twitter before leaving for work. I thought it was a joke, the jollity of the tweet hid the earth-shattering underbelly. Being a person that cannot think well without a pen, I dragged out a dog-eared pad and quickly checked the polynomials. That's when my gut finally realized that mathematics was going to die.

My head had had that realization a year before. I too had read the news that a machine had a gold medal level performance at the IMO. The publicly available, free models were capable of only proving very simple results, and so it was difficult to reconcile one's own experience with the abstract reality. Indeed, I was initially bullish that such models could automate the many, many creativity-free proofs occurring in homotopy theory and category theory - the ones I usually write "it is obvious" for. I don't know what I am saying: a lot can change in a year.

With this in mind, what are we to do?
